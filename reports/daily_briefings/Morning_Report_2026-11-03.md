👤 Alejandro Mozo Quesada, Sergio Gálvez Rojas | 📅 2026-10-31

**详情:** Character names in large language model (LLM) fiction concentrate on a handful of recurrent names such as 'Elara' and 'Elias', a measurable face of AI slop. Existing fixes either require logit access or retraining, or operate per request and cannot control concentration across a corpus. How much prompt-level strategies reduce concentration, and at what cost to name naturalness, has not been systematically measured. We present an empirical study of name-concentration control across eight strategies and introduce speculative naming, a fine-tuning-free method that adapts the draft-verify-reject paradigm of speculative decoding from tokens to names. The method requests candidate name-sets through verbalized sampling, scores them against a SlopTracker that combines a per-model fingerprint with a runtime counter, and selects through a temperature-controlled rule or its strict variant (HARD). We use 20 LLMs to generate 44,782 stories in four languages from text and visual stimuli (88,919 named characters), and measure concentration with a list-free collision metric (Slop Ratio, SR) decomposed into known and emergent components. List-based strategies do not remove slop: static banlists displace concentration to emergent names and more than halve the share of attested real names. Speculative naming removes it: the sampling scaffold alone cuts SR by 89% on average, the dynamic tracker brings the total reduction to 95%, mean collision probability falls from 7.3% to 0.4%, and known-name collisions hold at zero while naturalness stays close to baseline (42-69% attested by language) and within-story collisions drop from 4.3% to 0.2%. The strategy ranking is stable across languages, model families, and name-counting conventions. Name concentration is thus controllable at the corpus level without logit access or retraining; broader creative diversity beyond naming remains open.

**详情:** Knowledge graphs and ontologies form the backbone of structured, machine-readable knowledge representation, yet ontology terms often lack natural language descriptions, limiting their accessibility and reuse. Existing ontology verbalization approaches tend to rely on rule-based or template-driven techniques that operate on strict, formal schema axioms, resulting in rigid verbalizations that fail to convey richer contextual semantics. We present OntoDoc, a multi-agent framework for automatically generating natural language descriptions for any Internationalized Resource Identifier (IRI) within a SPARQL endpoint or RDF file. OntoDoc operates through a four-stage pipeline: (1) a Database Context agent that adaptively explores the knowledge graph via SPARQL to collect semantically informative triples about the target IRI; (2) a Web Context agent that retrieves and re-ranks encyclopedic background knowledge from Wikipedia and the open web; (3) an Assumption Generation agent that synthesizes both contexts into a structured, evidence-grounded list of intermediate assumptions; and (4) a Description Generation agent that produces the final natural language description from the grounded assumptions. We evaluate OntoDoc on three knowledge graphs spanning biomedical, agricultural, and cross-domain settings, using both automatic scoring via Large Language Models (LLMs) and human evaluation with 11 annotators. OntoDoc consistently matches or exceeds a naive LLM baseline and a template-based ontology verbalizer in accuracy. **详情:** As coding agents gain greater autonomy to modify software repositories, relevant context alone does not define their operational authority. An artifact may be relevant to a maintenance task while being inappropriate to edit; conversely, another artifact may be required even when it is not explicitly mentioned in the request. We explore Boundary Contracts, a machine-readable abstraction that makes task-specific artifact roles (editable, inspect-only, required, and protected) explicit before repository modification begins. This pilot does not establish the general effectiveness of Boundary Contracts.
